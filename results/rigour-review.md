# Adversarial review of this project's own claims

Generated by `src/rigour_check.py`. Every headline in this repo rests on a difference
between two numbers; this asks whether each difference is real.
Bootstrap: 2,000 resamples, percentile 95% CI.

  chat LLM  Brier 0.0746  = reliability 0.0012 - resolution 0.0071 + uncertainty 0.0817
            Brier skill vs always-guess-base-rate: +0.087

  NOTE the decomposition: reliability rewards honest probabilities, resolution
  rewards making distinctions. A wide-range model can lose on reliability and
  still be more useful. Judging calibration on mean-stated/truth alone is wrong.

  Jev range [0.00, 0.99]   chat LLM range [0.02, 0.42]

  in the overlap (67.7% of Jev's rows): Jev mean stated 0.028, actual 0.037  -> over by 0.77x
  whole sample: Jev over by 3.05x, DeepSeek over by 1.36x

==============================================================================
CLAIM 5: 'almost all the signal is age' (age alone AUC 0.7215)
==============================================================================
  age alone AUC 0.7215   Jev 0.7725   DeepSeek 0.7935
  Within narrow age bands, where age is nearly constant:
    ages 18-44: n=1,070  positives  12  Jev 0.764  DeepSeek 0.766
    ages 45-59: n=1,530  positives  89  Jev 0.803  DeepSeek 0.805
    ages 60-74: n=1,740  positives 217  Jev 0.715  DeepSeek 0.706
    ages 75+: n= 660  positives 131  Jev 0.704  DeepSeek 0.701

  This CONTRADICTS the 'it is all age' framing. AUC 0.70-0.80 within an age band
  is real discrimination among people the same age. Overall AUC understates that,
  because pooling lets any age-correlated score look good. The honest statement
  is that age is a strong predictor AND the models carry comparable non-age
  signal, which overall AUC partly hides.

  the earlier 'v3 ranks best' claim is not supported. It must be softened.

==============================================================================
CLAIM 8: complete-case selection bias
==============================================================================
  the derivation kept 253,680 of 441,456 raw records (57.5%) by dropping any respondent with a missing field.
  That is NOT random: refusals and don't-knows concentrate in the oldest and
  sickest respondents and in the youngest. The measured base rate is therefore
  a property of these 253,680 people, not of the US adult population.
  measured base rate here: 0.0942. CDC's own weighted
  figure for the same question is lower because it uses survey weights, which
  this analysis does not apply.
